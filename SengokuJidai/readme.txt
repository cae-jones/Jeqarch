Sengoku Jidai
("Warring States Era")
by C. A. E. Jones, 6/12/2011.


Note: This game is still in beta phase. Expect improvements
 with enough time and constructive feedback.


This game is intended to loosely simulate the military
 campaignes of feudal Japan, particularly around the warring
 states era (roughly the sixteenth century).
While attention has been given to historical details,
 don't expect a 100% accurate depiction of sixteenth century
 Japanese warfare.


----- Background Information -----

It is helpful to understand the Japanese feudal system
 to understand this game.
What follows is quite simplified, but covers what you need
 to know for this game.

The status of the Japanese monarchy has varied throughout history. Throughout the medieval period, up to the nineteenth century, the emperor had rather little power, and is basically irrelevant to many major events. This is due largely to the rise in power of military classes, notably the samurai, and the establishment of rule headed by military leaders.

These military leaders are called "shogun".
Samurai were the main military forces. Their position in society has varied over time. In the warring states era, the constant warfare made the samurai class much more open than it would become afterward. In times of peace, the samurai would often become burocrats or aristocrats, though in times of war their number were often recruited from commoners. At times, samurai would be discharged from their positions. Those that could not put away the warrior lifestyle became "ronan", samurai without a leader.

Feudal Japanese society was not simply shogun, samurai and commoners, however. There were rulers of clans and territories called "daimyou" (somewhat similar to the western concept of feudal lords). Daimyou were not inherently military leaders, but territorial disputes and clan warfare often put them in such positions, with samurai from their clan serving as soldiers.

The samurai developed a strong sense of honor and ideals that would eventually become "bushido". This strict honor code gave samurai dedication and proficiency at their careers, but it meant that most would not dare take on tasks such as spying, sabotage, or assassination. With constant warfare between powerful clans, though, many had need of such unseamly tasks. Those that rose to fill these positions were called "shinobi" (more commonly "ninja" nowadays). Shinobi were particularly useful in sabotaging enemy camps and fortresses, and most of our historical records of shinobi in warfare involve their sabotage of castles.

----- Game Play -----


---------- general ----------

Initially, you will control a comparatively weak shogun with a rather small army.
You can switch control to any character under your shogun's leadership whenever you wish. If your shogun dies, however, you have lost the game.

Shogun can participate in battles, but their greatest strength is in being able to command anyone who follows them, including daimyou, samurai, commoners, and hired shinobi.

A shogun's holdings grow through conquest or coersion. If your army kills an enemy shogun, anyone that followed that shogun will either commit suicide, or join you (sometimes, conquered samurai will go ronan instead of changing sides or committing sepuku).
The same will happen if a daimyou is slain--all of that daimyou's underlings will either join you, commit suicide, or become ronan.
Generally, samurai and daimyou are more likely to commit suicide than commoners or shinobi.

You can at times gain allies without killing. Disgruntled commoners, ronan, shinobi, and even some daimyou can be persuaded to join you at the offer of resources or other compensation.

Generally speaking, your goal is to conquer all of Japan.
 Wiping out opposing shogun, and bringing any stray daimyou
 under your control will usually accomplish this.

(In the future, there may be more game modes, such as
 campaignes or online play.)

---------- Character Types ----------

Shogun: You only have access to one of these, and it is the character you control when starting a new game. Shogun generally have all of the equipment common to samurai, and can give orders to anyone beneath them, regardless of that underling's class.

Daimyou: Daimyou are not as heavily armed as samurai or shogun, but are aware enough of the constant danger to their lives, and therefore carry a few weapons in the event that they need to fight (or if they wind up in a situation where death seems more appealing than dishonor). A daimyou can give commands to samurai, commoners or shinobi under him, but not to other daimyou or their followers, even if they serve the same shogun. Daimyou will relay the orders of their shogun (if they have one) to their underlings. (So it is possible for a shogun to divide up his forces by giving different orders to each daimyou under his command).

Samurai: These do the bulk of the fighting, and therefore carry the most powerful weapons. They will obey their daimyou and shogun to the death, typically will not flee from a fight, and are least likely to switch sides if conquered (despite that they are most valuable to the conquering leaders...!).

Civilians: These common people avoid combat, but many carry small weapons for defense. These cultivate vital resources that armies need to survive, however, andn should not be ignored. On occasion, some civilians might become samurai or ronan, though this is relatively rare. Civilians generally obey the commands of daimyou or shogun, but are more worried about survival than the honor code of the samurai, so are a bit less reliable.

Shinobi: These typically are independent agents, but once contracted, are just as loyal as the samurai. Their specialty is stealth, infiltration and assassination, so their weapons are better suited for attacks from secrecy than for open combat. Shinobi are capable of fleeing attackers, and can hide in places that other characters cannot (such as in holes, trees, under water, etc). Shinobi are masters of disguise, and can sometimes convince enemies that they are civilians or samurai of their own army.

Ronan: Ronan are not playable, since by their nature they are not part of your army. They have most of the weapons of the samurai, though since they are out of a job they can sometimes be lacking propper shields, armor or arrows. Some ronan can be convinced to join you, though, at which point they become samurai in your army.

It is worth noting that characters are named. These names are randomly generated, and are used only to help identify characters when one has a large army, or needs to recognize certain groups.

One can press the tab key during play to bring up the character select menu. Press tab again or escape to cancel the selection.
The left and right arrow keys can be used to limit your selection to a particular class (samurai, civilians and shinobi are further subdivided into groups based on their daimyou).
Use the up and down arrow keys to select an individual, and press enter to switch control to that character.

If you're wondering why you'd ever want to change characters (since the shogun can fight just as well as a samurai), then consider a thing called strategy.
For starters, the shogun is the heart of your army. It is a great risk to throw him into the heat of battle.
At the same time, it is dangerous to sit by and let the computer attempt to successfully carry out the shogun's orders without problems.

And surely the idea of playing as a samurai or ninja is a little appealing? :P

By changing which characters you control, you can experience many aspects of the struggles, and can have a greater degree of influence over what happens. You can have your shogun order one daimyou to lead an attack on a castle, and order another to cut off resources to the castle. But what if in this violence, you wind up killing quite a few enemy troops, and have some civilians that could use them and thus expand your combat strength? Or, what if you are low on workers, and want them to avoid picking up fallen weapons?


---------- Combat ----------

While there are various types of weapons available, they come in two basic varieties: Projectile and melee.
Generally speaking, unarmed combat is not allowed, but sometimes happens if a character is disarmed.

To select a weapon, one uses the number keys, starting at 1. The maximum number of weapons will vary depending on the character's class; samurai have as many as 5 to choose from, while most commoners will only have one. To hear what weapon the active character has drawn, press w.

One prepares an attack by pressing the space bar.
Holding the space bar can increase the effectiveness of an attack, but leaves the attacker vulnerable while the button is held.

In the case of projectile weapons, such as the yumi or shuriken, attacking costs ammunition, which can be replenished by locating fallen projectiles, or by crafting new ones (typically from wood). Projectile weapons have long range, but have no defensive abilities, and they are usually deflected by armor and shields (though occasionally a well-placed shot will get through armor).
Projectile weapons have no serious recovery upon use, and do not make one fatigued.

Melee weapons have shorter range, and constant swinging of them can cause one to become fatigued and slow down. They can often do more damage than projectiles, and have no ammunition cost. In addition, melee attacks can block other melee attacks. Generally speaking, if two melee attacks collide, the weapons will clash and neither combattant will take damage. Therefore, melee combat is largely dependent on strategic timing.

Aside from range, different weapons can accomplish different things; for instance, the kanabo can break through shields. Larger weapons are slower and have a greater fatigue cost, however.

You can tell when characters are fatigued not only because they will slow, but they will start breathing heavily.
Fatigue discipates with rest, which is more effective in houses.

---------- Navigation ----------

When in normal play, the arrow keys will move the active character in the given direction if possible.

To examine one's surroundings, one can hold shift and press a directional key to get a description of what lies in that direction within a certain distance. Keep in mind that some objects can hide others from sight--for instance, you can peer around a tree, but the tree will still obscure what is closest to it if the tree is directly in front of you. Also, shinobi can hide in many places, and you won't be able to find them by this method when they are hiding.
 (If you want to silence the description, press control; if you want to skip to the next item being listed, press shift.)

Some structures hold additional details that can only be discerned at close range--for instance, if there is any useful brush or fruit in a tree, or if a wall has arrow-slits or handholds.

Some degree of echo location and ambience shifting should be used to give an idea of approaching obstacles, but keep in mind that the terrain varies--for instance, mountains are generally going to echo even without you approaching a wall, and planes are generally quieter in terms of ambience. It is also worth noting that ambience is lost when one is inside of a castle.

---------- Interaction ----------

The enter key is the primary means of interacting with one's environment. Pressing enter will cause the character to attempt to collect any items at their current location; is the primary method for shinobi-specific maneuvers such as hiding or climbing; can be used to initiate offers toward non-allies.

---------- Giving Orders ----------

To start giving orders, press the o key. Note that you can only give orders to people that are within a certain range. The game will pause as you select who to give the orders to ("everyone" is the default), and which orders to give.

Generally, if any of your underlings do not have orders and are not in a castle (or are civilians / shinobi in a village), they will tend to follow their leader.

Note that if you've already given orders, and try to give new orders, the new orders will replace the old ones.

enter - general interaction
shift + arrows - look in a given direction.
shift + enter - limited view of the surroundings as a grid.

 space bar - attack

m - Map overview.

 shift + / - Speak how many players are online (multiplayer only)

 page up - music and ambience louder
 page down - music and ambience softer
shift + left bracket - game speed slower
 shift + right bracket - game speed faster


----- Display Key

As of version 1.5, this game supports outputting a string representing what is nearby to the title bar of the window.
For some screen readers, this change will be recognized, and can be sent to a braille display.
It is known to work for NVDA; any other screen readers are untested.

The display has three settings.
Off: The display will not be used.
Only while looking: When using the look command (shift + enter), or while in certain menus or typing messages, the display will output.
On: The display will be updated during every second of game play.

The symbols used in the display are as follows. Note that people will cover up the tile on which they stand:

-  = clear
=  = castle wall
'  = castle room
,  = courtyard
g  = garden
t  = tree
o  = hole
B  = boulder
w  = water
W  = deep water
H  = house
E  = horse
A  = boat
F  = fire
0  = broken wall
C = campsite
S = samurai
%  = shogun
D  = daimyou
N  = shinobi
R  = Ronan
M  = man
W  = woman
